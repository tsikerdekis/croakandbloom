intro: Each piece from Croak and Bloom is the product of seven quiet steps. There are no shortcuts — the wheel, the kiln, and the slow accumulation of time do most of the work.
steps:
  - title: Wedging
    image: /assets/images/process-01-wedging.jpg
    alt: Hands working clay at the pottery wheel
    description: Before a single piece is thrown, the stoneware clay is wedged by hand to remove air bubbles and bring the moisture to a uniform consistency. It is meditative, physical work — the first quiet conversation with the material.

  - title: Throwing
    image: /assets/images/process-02-throwing.jpg
    alt: Clay being centered and pulled up on the pottery wheel
    description: Forms are centered on the wheel and pulled up from a single ball of clay — mugs, bowls, vases. The shapes you see in the finished work are decided in these few minutes, mostly by feel.

  - title: Trimming
    image: /assets/images/process-03-trimming.jpg
    alt: A freshly trimmed ceramic piece on the wheel
    description: After the piece stiffens to leather-hard, it is inverted and trimmed on the wheel. Excess clay is removed from the foot, the wall is refined, and a small inscription — C&B — is stamped into the base.

  - title: Drying
    image: /assets/images/process-04-drying.jpg
    alt: Small wheel-thrown pots drying on the workbench
    description: Pieces dry slowly under cloth for several days, then uncovered, until they reach bone-dry. Rushing this step invites cracking. Patience is a material.

  - title: Glazing
    image: /assets/images/process-05-glazing.jpg
    alt: Hand-carved stoneware hearts with brilliant blue glaze
    description: Each piece is hand-dipped or brushed with one of our satin matte glazes — soft pinks, mint, lavender, sun-yellow, and quiet neutrals. Glaze is a chemistry of silica, clay, and mineral oxides, mixed in small batches in the studio.

  - title: Firing
    image: /assets/images/process-06-firing.jpg
    alt: A cone-6 kiln firing glowing orange-hot
    description: The kiln runs to cone 6, about 2232°F, over a fourteen-hour cycle. Each firing is loaded and unloaded by hand, and the cooling takes another full day. A piece in the kiln is a piece in suspension.

  - title: Finishing
    image: /assets/images/process-07-finishing.jpg
    alt: Finished pottery pieces on a shelf in the studio
    description: Once cooled, every foot is sanded smooth, every piece is inspected, and any that fall short of the studio's standard is broken down and returned to the clay reclaim. What remains is what you see in the shop.
