intro: Each piece from Croak and Bloom is the product of six quiet steps. There are no shortcuts. The wheel, the kiln, and the slow accumulation of time do most of the work.
steps:
  - title: Throwing
    image: /assets/images/process-02-throwing.jpg
    alt: Anna at the pottery wheel, holding a ball of clay in each hand before centering it
    description: Forms are centered on the wheel and pulled up from a single ball of clay. Mugs, bowls, vases, the shapes you see in the finished work are decided in these few minutes, mostly by feel.

  - title: Trimming
    image: /assets/images/process-03-trimming.jpg
    anim: /assets/images/process-03-trimming.webp
    alt: An inverted leather-hard pot on the wheel, showing the stamped C&B frog mark on its foot
    description: After the piece stiffens to leather-hard, it is inverted and trimmed on the wheel. Excess clay is removed from the foot, the wall is refined, and a small C&B inscription is stamped into the base.

  - title: Drying
    image: /assets/images/process-04-drying.jpg
    alt: Freshly thrown bowls and cups drying on a board in the studio
    description: Pieces dry slowly under cloth for several days, then uncovered, until they reach bone-dry. Rushing this step invites cracking. Patience is a material.

  - title: Glazing
    image: /assets/images/process-05-glazing.jpg
    anim: /assets/images/process-05-glazing.webp
    alt: Hands brushing white glaze onto a small hand-built ceramic spoon
    description: Each piece is hand-dipped or brushed with one of our satin matte glazes in soft pinks, mint, lavender, sun-yellow, and quiet neutrals. Glaze is a chemistry of silica, clay, and mineral oxides, mixed in small batches in the studio.

  - title: Firing
    image: /assets/images/process-06-firing.jpg
    alt: An open kiln showing a shelf of freshly fired glazed mugs in pink, mint, and grey
    description: The kiln runs to cone 6, about 2232°F, over a fourteen-hour cycle. Each firing is loaded and unloaded by hand, and the cooling takes another full day. A piece in the kiln is a piece in suspension.

  - title: Finishing
    image: /assets/images/process-07-finishing.jpg
    alt: Finished pottery pieces on a shelf in the studio
    description: Once cooled, every foot is sanded smooth, every piece is inspected, and any that fall short of the studio's standard is broken down and returned to the clay reclaim. What remains is what you see in the shop.
